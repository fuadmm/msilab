## 2. Academic Thesis & Research Defense
* **Thesis Title:** "A Mystic Exploration of JEV Immunogenicity: Anti JEV-ED3 Antibody Responses Against Wild-type & Destabilizing Mutants of JEV-ED3"
* **Primary Supervisor:** Prof. Dr. Mohammad Monirul Islam
* **Academic Session / Level:** MS Session 2023–2024
* **Submission / Defense Date:** June 7, 2026
* **Scientific Abstract:**
> Japanese encephalitis virus (JEV), a positive-sense single-stranded RNA flavivirus, is a major cause of endemic viral encephalitis. Its envelope protein domain III (JEV-ED3) plays a crucial role in receptor binding, membrane fusion, and the induction of neutralizing antibodies, making it a promising subunit vaccine candidate. In this study, anti-JEV-ED3 antibody responses were evaluated against Escherichia coli-expressed wild-type and structurally modified variants in a murine model. The wild-type JEV-ED3 sequence was obtained from UniProt and synthesized by Eurofins, while destabilized variants (JEV-ED3-C5I, -P47A, -P47G, and -F21A) were generated through site-directed mutagenesis and confirmed by DNA sequencing. All variants were expressed in E. coli BL21 as inclusion bodies. BALB/c mice were immunized subcutaneously with three biweekly doses (15 µg/mouse) of JEV-ED3-WT (with or without 5 µM cobalt) or adjuvant-free mutant variants. IgG responses were measured using indirect ELISA following tail bleeding, and immune marker expression was analyzed via one-step RT-qPCR. All recombinant variants were found to be non-toxic and immunogenic. A marked increase in antibody titers was observed after the third immunization in groups receiving WT, WT-Co, C5I, and F21A variants, whereas P47A and P47G variants showed minimal responses. Notably, JEV-ED3-WT supplemented with cobalt (WT-Co) and the F21A variant produced the highest antibody titers, exhibiting more than 11-fold and 8-fold increases, respectively, compared to the wild-type protein. Despite differences in immunogenicity, all variants predominantly induced a Th2-skewed humoral response, characterized by elevated IgG1 levels. Furthermore, RT-qPCR analysis demonstrated a positive correlation between higher anti-ED3 antibody titers and increased expression of key immune markers, including CD86, TLR4, CD80, and MHC class II. These findings indicate that the incorporation of trace cobalt or the addition of five C-terminal isoleucine residues (C5I) significantly enhances the relatively low immunogenicity of wild-type JEV-ED3. Additionally, structural destabilization via the F21A mutation improved immunogenicity, whereas P47A and P47G mutations had no significant effect. Overall, this study highlights the importance of the stability–immunogenicity relationship and underscores the need for further research to rationally design effective subunit vaccines against emerging JEV variants.
